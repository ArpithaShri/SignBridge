\section{Spatial Representation versus Temporal Recurrence}
\label{sec:spatial_vs_temporal}
A central finding of this investigation is that \textbf{spatial representation fidelity is the dominant determinant of recognition success} under limited training data. In dynamic sign language, keyframes corresponding to gesture apexes contain distinctive spatial signatures. Because MobileNetV2 was pre-trained on ImageNet, its frozen convolutional filters reliably detect hand contours and orientations without prior sign training. Temporal probability averaging over 33 frames effectively acts as a majority voting filter, filtering out transitional motion blur while amplifying discriminative apex frames (67.78\% accuracy).

Conversely, the scratch-trained CNN-LSTM attempted to learn 2D spatial feature representations and 128-unit recurrent temporal transitions simultaneously on only 806 training videos. The TimeDistributed convolutional layers failed to converge on clean spatial boundaries, feeding degraded representations into the LSTM and resulting in 4.44\% test accuracy. This demonstrates that recurrent sequence modeling requires either pre-trained visual encoders or massive video training corpora.

\section{Transfer Learning and Inference Efficiency}
\label{sec:transfer_learning_efficiency}
Freezing MobileNetV2's 2.26M backbone parameters provided three key advantages: (1) immunity against overfitting on background artifacts, (2) rapid convergence adjusting only 347K head parameters, and (3) lightweight CPU serving ($\approx 140$\,ms latency), eliminating the need for dedicated server GPUs.

\section{Late Fusion Dynamics and System Generalization}
\label{sec:fusion_generalization}
The late-fusion experiment illustrates that ensemble averaging is detrimental when constituent models exhibit large competence disparities ($\text{Acc}_{\text{mob}} = 67.78\% > \text{Acc}_{\text{fusion}} = 60.56\%$). Uncalibrated predictions from the scratch models inject false probability mass into the ensemble distribution.

Regarding generalization, while SignBridge achieves 67.78\% accuracy on the sequestered 180-video test split, performance in uncontrolled real-world environments with severe illumination shifts, cluttered backgrounds, or unconstrained camera angles requires ongoing validation across broader signer cohorts.
