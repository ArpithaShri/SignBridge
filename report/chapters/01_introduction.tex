\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}
Sign language constitutes a natural, visual-gestural linguistic system serving as the primary medium of communication for millions of deaf and hard-of-hearing individuals. In India, an estimated 5 to 10 million individuals rely on Indian Sign Language (ISL) \cite{kumar2018indian}. ISL conveys semantics through manual articulators (handshapes, spatial locations, and movement trajectories) combined with non-manual markers (facial expressions and torso postures) \cite{rastgoo2021sign, adaloglou2022comprehensive}. However, a persistent shortage of certified interpreters limits social, educational, and professional inclusion for deaf individuals. Automating sign recognition from standard video offers a scalable assistive bridge \cite{starner1998real, camgoz2018neural}.

\section{Problem Statement}
\label{sec:problem_statement}
Dynamic sign language recognition faces severe challenges: high inter-class visual similarity (minimal gesture pairs), non-linear temporal execution phases (preparation, stroke, retraction), unconstrained recording environments, and acute training data scarcity compared to general computer vision benchmarks \cite{deng2009imagenet, carreira2017quo}. The research problem addressed in this case study is: \textit{How can deep learning architectures reliably recognize dynamic ISL gestures across 76 discrete word classes under limited-data constraints, and how can such models be deployed into an interactive, low-latency web application?}

\section{Motivation}
\label{sec:motivation}
Developing a vision-based recognition system that relies strictly on standard monocular 2D RGB video eliminates reliance on expensive or intrusive physical sensor gloves and depth sensors \cite{adaloglou2022comprehensive}. Investigating how transfer-learning spatial backbones compare against scratch-trained 2D convolutional and recurrent sequence networks provides critical empirical guidance for real-world assistive AI.

\section{Objectives}
\label{sec:objectives}
The core objectives of the SignBridge project are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Standardize a 76-class dynamic ISL video corpus (1,166 clips) into 806 training, 180 validation, and 180 held-out test samples with uniform 33-frame temporal sampling.
    \item Implement, train, and benchmark three deep learning architectures: MobileNetV2 (frozen transfer learning), Custom 2D CNN (scratch baseline), and CNN-LSTM (spatio-temporal).
    \item Conduct rigorous empirical evaluation on the 180-video held-out test split.
    \item Build and deploy a full-stack web application featuring a FastAPI REST microservice with in-memory model caching and a responsive React 19 / TypeScript user interface.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Scope of the Project}
\label{sec:scope}
The project encompasses isolated recognition of 76 dynamic ISL words from RGB video files (\texttt{.MOV}, \texttt{.mp4}, \texttt{.avi}, \texttt{.webm}) and in-browser webcam recording. 
\textit{Scope Exclusions:} Continuous sign translation, fingerspelling of arbitrary text, and generative data augmentation experiments (e.g., GANs or Lab Evaluation 2 modules) are strictly excluded.

\section{Contributions}
\label{sec:contributions}
The primary contributions of this work are: (1) an end-to-end hardware-agnostic recognition framework, (2) rigorous comparative benchmarking demonstrating transfer learning's superiority (67.78\%) over scratch models ($<5.00\%$), (3) an automated cross-model consensus and agreement engine, (4) a production FastAPI backend with in-memory singleton weight caching, and (5) an accessible modern web interface.
